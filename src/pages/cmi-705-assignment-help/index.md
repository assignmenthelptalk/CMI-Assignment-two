## What CMI Unit 705 covers

According to the CMI Level 7 syllabus (August 2026), Unit 705 is worth 8 credits, with 24 guided learning hours and 80 hours of total unit time. It has 2 learning outcomes and 5 assessment criteria, and every criterion has to be met for the unit to be passed.

Keywords the syllabus lists for the unit: Change, drivers, scope, context, problem-solving, theory, approaches, strategy, reflection, success.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

### Learning outcome 1: Understand the scope and context of strategic change

- 1.1 **Discuss** the scope, context and drivers for organisational change
- 1.2 **Critically appraise** the complexities of leading strategic change
- 1.3 **Critically evaluate** theories and models for leading and managing strategic change

### Learning outcome 2: Know how to propose a strategy for leading strategic change

- 2.1 **Develop** a proposal for leading strategic change
- 2.2 **Reflect** on how approaches to leadership can be applied to deliver the strategy for change

## What the command verbs ask for in this unit

The first word of each criterion tells you the depth required. The verbs used in Unit 705 are:

- **Discuss** (1.1): give a detailed account that includes a range of views, including contrasting perspectives.
- **Critically appraise** (1.2): use a systematic process to find the strengths and weaknesses of information and judge how useful and valid it is.
- **Critically evaluate** (1.3): weigh strengths and weaknesses and arguments for and against, then reach a reasoned judgement supported by current research or theory.
- **Develop** (2.1): elaborate and progress an idea from a starting point, building on the information given.
- **Reflect** (2.2): consciously contemplate your actions or an issue and give a balanced view.

These are plain-English readings of the verbs. Your centre's assessment brief and its assessor have the final say on what counts as a pass.

## How to approach a Unit 705 assignment

- AC1.3 asks you to critically evaluate theories and models, so compare at least two against the same change problem and say where each helps and fails.
- AC2.1 is a proposal, so include an outline of the change, anticipated benefits and timeframe and resources, as the indicative content describes, and tie the proposal to the models you evaluated.
- AC2.2 asks how leadership approaches deliver the change strategy. Reflect on which approaches suit which stage of change rather than naming one favourite.

## Where Unit 705 assignments go wrong

- Retelling Kotter's eight steps in AC1.3 instead of evaluating the model's strengths and limits.
- Describing change drivers in AC1.1 without discussing scope, such as whole organisation versus a single team.
- Making a proposal in AC2.1 with no risks or resistance, although the indicative content lists organisational culture and resistance to change.

## How CMI suggests Unit 705 can be assessed

CMI says all assessment criteria must be met, and that the following ways of assessing the unit are guidance only. Your centre chooses the format, so follow your own brief:

- Write a report on the scope, context and complexity of organisational change
- Develop a proposal for a strategy to lead change in an organisational context
- Produce a reflective account on their leadership practice to evidence their ability to meet each of the assessment criteria

CMI Level 7 qualifications are graded Pass or Refer. The guideline word count for this unit is 4,500 words, with a 10% allowance above or below, so plan your sections around that.

## CMI Unit 705 FAQ

**Which change model should I use for Unit 705?**

CMI does not require one. The indicative content cites Kotter, Lewin, McKinsey 7S, Appreciative Enquiry and others as examples, so pick models that fit your change and justify the choice.

**Can I write about a change I have already led?**

CMI's recommendations include a reflective account of leading change, and the unit's LO2 criteria ask you to propose and reflect, so a real case can work. Your centre's brief decides the format.

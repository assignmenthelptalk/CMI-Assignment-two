## What CMI Unit 602 Covers

Unit 602 addresses the senior manager's role in leading people and developing organisational capability. The learning outcomes require you to critically evaluate leadership approaches for developing people, analyse the role of senior leaders in building organisational capability, and critically evaluate the conditions that enable effective leadership development. At Level 6, the Critically Evaluate standard requires examining not just what each framework recommends but what assumptions it rests on and where competing frameworks provide a more complete account.

## Bass and Avolio's Transformational Leadership — Critically Evaluate

Bernard Bass and Bruce Avolio's Transformational Leadership model (1994, *Improving Organizational Effectiveness Through Transformational Leadership*, Sage) identifies four dimensions — the Four I's — through which transformational leaders develop and motivate followers: **Idealised Influence** (the leader acts as a role model, inspiring trust and admiration through integrity and performance); **Inspirational Motivation** (the leader articulates a compelling vision that motivates followers to transcend self-interest for collective goals); **Intellectual Stimulation** (the leader challenges followers' assumptions and encourages innovative thinking and creative problem-solving); **Individualised Consideration** (the leader attends to the individual developmental needs of each follower, acting as mentor and coach).

**Critically Evaluate — application**: transformational leadership applied to Leading and Developing People means examining how each of the Four I's is enacted in a senior management development context. Intellectual Stimulation is particularly significant at senior team level — senior managers require challenge to assumptions, exposure to different perspectives, and opportunities to engage with complexity, not primarily direction and support. Individualised Consideration is the development dimension: the senior manager as developer of individuals, attending to each team member's specific developmental needs rather than applying a uniform development programme.

**Critically Evaluate — assumptions and limitations**: transformational leadership rests on the assumption that leaders can authentically enact all four I's — that inspirational vision, role-model integrity, intellectual challenge, and individual attention can coexist in the same leader behaviour set. This is a demanding assumption. Research by Lowe et al. (1996, *The Leadership Quarterly*, 7(3)) found that transformational leadership effects are largest in military and public sector settings and smallest in business settings — raising questions about the universality of the model's applicability.

The most significant assumption the model makes for development contexts: Individualised Consideration assumes that the leader has sufficient time, insight, and developmental capability to genuinely attend to each team member's individual needs. At senior management level with wide spans of control, this assumption is structurally tested — a Director with 12 direct reports who each require genuinely individualised developmental attention faces a resource constraint that the model does not address.

**Competing framework**: Spillane's Distributed Leadership provides a complementary account that transformational leadership does not capture: where transformational leadership focuses on the leader's behaviours, distributed leadership examines how leadership for development is distributed across the organisation — through peer mentoring, communities of practice, and structured development programmes that do not depend on any single leader's individual Individualised Consideration. The two frameworks are not mutually exclusive but address different levels of the development system.

## Spillane's Distributed Leadership for People Development — Critically Evaluate

Distributed leadership (Spillane, 2006) applied to people development argues that organisational capability is built through distributed leadership activity — multiple senior managers and informal leaders contributing to the development of others, rather than development being a function exclusively of formal line management.

**Critically Evaluate — assumptions**: the distributed leadership assumption for people development is that informal leadership activity — coaching conversations between colleagues, communities of practice, peer challenge — constitutes genuine leadership for development and has measurable impact on organisational capability. This assumption is empirically supported in educational settings (Spillane's primary research context) but the evidence base in complex commercial and public sector organisations is less developed. A Critically Evaluate response engages with this evidence gap rather than citing Spillane as if the educational research context transfers automatically.

**Defensible conclusion**: for people development at senior management level, transformational leadership (Bass and Avolio) provides the most applicable individual leadership framework — because the Four I's directly address the behavioural repertoire of a senior manager acting as developer of other leaders. Distributed leadership (Spillane) provides the most applicable system-level framework — because capability building at scale requires distributed contributions that no single transformational leader can provide. The Critically Evaluate conclusion at Level 6 is not "transformational leadership is best" but "transformational leadership provides the most applicable individual-level framework for this context; distributed leadership provides the system architecture within which individual transformational leadership is operationalised and scaled." The two frameworks address different questions and are complementary rather than competing at the implementation level.

## Organisational Capability Building — Critically Evaluate

Beyond individual leadership development, Unit 602 addresses how senior managers build organisational capability — the collective capacity of the organisation to deliver its purpose and adapt to changing demands. At Level 6 Critically Evaluate depth: examine what "organisational capability" means beyond individual skill development, and what assumptions underlie common capability-building approaches.

The systematic capability-building framework at Level 6 includes: **succession planning** (developing the leadership pipeline); **talent management** (identifying and accelerating the development of high-potential individuals); **communities of practice** (Wenger, 1998, *Communities of Practice*, Cambridge University Press — shared learning through sustained mutual engagement among practitioners); and **organisational learning** (Senge, 1990, *The Fifth Discipline*, Doubleday — systems thinking as the foundation for learning organisations). Each of these approaches rests on assumptions that require Critically Evaluate examination: talent management assumes that high potential is identifiable early and that identified talent develops as expected — research suggests both assumptions are empirically fragile.

## Pass / Merit / Distinction

**Pass:** Bass and Avolio's Four I's and Spillane's Distributed Leadership applied to a people development context. Assumptions of each framework named. A peer-reviewed source included. Advanced management paper format.

**Merit:** Assumptions of transformational leadership examined specifically — which I is most applicable, which is most constrained by the specific context. Distributed leadership evaluated against transformational leadership in the same development context. Evidence from peer-reviewed research cited in the analysis, not just the bibliography.

**Distinction — worked example:** "The Critically Evaluate analysis of transformational and distributed leadership for senior team development in the ICS context reveals a structural tension that neither framework fully resolves. Bass and Avolio's Individualised Consideration — the developmental I most directly applicable to people development — assumes dyadic leader-to-follower development relationships. In an ICS system where system leadership is explicitly distributed across NHS trusts, local authorities, and voluntary sector partners, no single leader has the authority or proximity to provide Individualised Consideration to all the senior leaders whose development the system requires. Spillane's distributed leadership appears to resolve this: system-level leadership for development is distributed across the ICS partner organisations. However, Spillane's framework does not address the accountability question that the ICS governance architecture requires — who is responsible when distributed leadership for development produces uneven results across partner organisations? The defensible conclusion is that neither framework alone provides adequate guidance for ICS-level people development: Bass and Avolio's Individualised Consideration must be operationalised within each partner organisation by its senior leaders; Spillane's distributed approach provides the architecture for cross-organisational leadership development programmes (leadership fellows, secondments, joint communities of practice) that transcend individual organisational boundaries. The SMART recommendation is the development of a cross-ICS leadership development programme operationalising Spillane's distributed approach, with each partner organisation's executive team responsible for transformational leadership development within their own organisation."

## Common Questions About CMI Unit 602

**What are the Four I's of transformational leadership and how do I Critically Evaluate them?**
The Four I's are: Idealised Influence, Inspirational Motivation, Intellectual Stimulation, and Individualised Consideration. Critically Evaluating them means examining what each assumes. Inspirational Motivation assumes followers are motivated by a compelling vision — but empirical research (Judge and Piccolo, 2004, *Journal of Applied Psychology*, 89(5)) found that idealized influence and inspirational motivation may measure similar underlying constructs, raising questions about the distinctiveness of the four dimensions. Individualised Consideration assumes the leader can provide genuinely individualised developmental attention — but wide spans of control make this structurally constrained. Intellectual Stimulation assumes followers are ready and willing to have their assumptions challenged — but this is a cultural condition that the framework does not address. Critically Evaluating the Four I's means engaging with these specific assumptions, not just describing what each I involves.

**How is Unit 602 different from Unit 502 (Level 5)?**
Unit 502 at Level 5 applies transformational and transactional leadership at Evaluate depth — examining the frameworks' usefulness and limitations and forming a supported judgement. Unit 602 at Level 6 Critically Evaluates the same frameworks — examining their theoretical assumptions, engaging with peer-reviewed research evidence, and considering competing frameworks (Spillane's distributed leadership) that are not typically addressed at Level 5. The Level 6 response is also longer (4,000–5,000 words vs 2,500–3,500 at Level 5) and must include peer-reviewed journals.

**What peer-reviewed sources should I use for Unit 602?**

**Is servant leadership (Greenleaf) also relevant to Unit 602?**
Servant leadership is the primary framework for Unit 601 and is less central to Unit 602's people development focus. However, where the development context involves creating a culture of psychological safety and follower empowerment, Greenleaf's servant leadership provides relevant framing. Check your specific brief — if it mentions servant leadership, include it; if it focuses on development and capability building, Bass and Avolio's Individualised Consideration is the more directly applicable framework.

**How should SMART recommendations in a Level 6 assignment differ from Level 5?**
At Level 5, SMART recommendations follow from Evaluate analysis — they are specific, measurable, and time-bound interventions traceable to the evaluation. At Level 6, SMART recommendations must be explicitly traceable to the Critically Evaluate conclusions — they should name which framework's evidence supports them and acknowledge the theoretical assumptions the recommendation depends on. A Level 6 SMART recommendation does not just state "implement a leadership development programme by Q2" but specifies the theoretical basis ("consistent with Bass and Avolio's Individualised Consideration — each senior manager to conduct quarterly structured development conversations using a coaching model"), the metric, the timeline, and the governance accountability.

# CMI Unit 407 Assignment Help — Conducting a Management Project

CMI Unit 407, Conducting a Management Project, covers project management methodology and stakeholder management for managers who initiate, plan, and oversee projects as part of their management role. Submitted as a management report at Level 4 Analyse and Evaluate depth, it applies the project lifecycle, Gantt charts, and Mendelow's Stakeholder Matrix to plan and manage a specific project. Unlike most Level 4 units, Unit 407 typically produces a management report on an actual or proposed project, making it one of the most applied and practical units in the qualification. ## What CMI Unit 407 Covers

Unit 407 addresses project management as a management competency. The learning outcomes require you to develop a project plan using appropriate project management tools, analyse and manage project stakeholders, evaluate the risks associated with the project, and conduct the project with reference to the management plan. Unusually for Level 4, many Unit 407 briefs require an actual project proposal or project report rather than a purely theoretical essay, the assessment is about applying project management tools to a real management scenario.

## The Project Management Lifecycle — Analyse Depth

The project management lifecycle provides the structural framework for managing a project from inception to closure. The four-stage lifecycle:

**Initiation:** define the project: what is the objective, what is the scope (what is included and excluded), what are the success criteria, what resources are required, who are the key stakeholders, and what is the authority structure? The project initiation document (PID) or project brief formalises these decisions. At Level 4 Analyse depth: analyse what happens when Initiation is rushed or incomplete. Scope creep, the gradual expansion of project scope beyond the original brief, is the most common cause of project failure, and it almost always originates in incomplete scope definition at Initiation. A project that does not explicitly define what is out of scope will have its boundaries continuously renegotiated as stakeholders add requirements.

**Planning:** develop the detailed plan: work breakdown structure (WBS), task sequencing, resource allocation, timeline, risk register, communication plan, and stakeholder engagement plan. This is where Gantt charts and Mendelow's matrix are applied. At Analyse depth: analyse the critical path, the sequence of tasks whose cumulative duration determines the minimum project duration. Tasks on the critical path cannot be delayed without extending the overall project timeline; tasks off the critical path have float, time they can slip without affecting the overall timeline.

**Execution (Delivery):** implement the plan: manage resources, monitor progress against the plan, manage stakeholder relationships, resolve issues, and control scope. At Analyse depth: the key management challenge in Execution is not doing the work but managing the gap between the plan and reality. The plan becomes a baseline for comparison, not a rigid script, the skilled project manager monitors variances, makes decisions about which variances require corrective action, and updates the plan when circumstances change.

**Closure:** formally close the project: confirm that objectives have been met, obtain stakeholder sign-off, document lessons learned, release resources, and transition deliverables to operational ownership. At Analyse depth: the closure stage is routinely under-resourced and under-managed. Lessons-learned processes are frequently omitted under time pressure, which means project experience is lost rather than captured for future application.

## Gantt Charts — Analyse Depth

A Gantt chart is a bar chart that displays project tasks against a timeline, showing planned start and end dates for each task, dependencies between tasks, and progress against the plan. At Level 4 Analyse depth, the Gantt chart is not just a scheduling tool, it is a communication and monitoring tool that makes the project plan visible to all stakeholders and enables variance tracking.

At Analyse depth: the critical path is visible on a Gantt chart as the longest sequence of dependent tasks. Tasks on the critical path have no float, they must start and finish on time for the project to complete on time. At Evaluate depth: evaluate which tasks in your specific project are on the critical path and require the most intensive monitoring. A project where the critical path runs through a task dependent on a specific external supplier (whose delivery is outside the project manager's control) has an externally held critical path risk that requires specific mitigation in the risk register.

The work breakdown structure (WBS) underpins the Gantt chart, before tasks can be plotted on a timeline, they must be identified through WBS, which breaks the project deliverables into progressively smaller packages of work until each package is manageable by a named resource. At Level 4, including a WBS table and Gantt chart in the management report appendix, with analytical reference to the critical path in the body, demonstrates tool application at Analyse depth.

## Mendelow's Stakeholder Matrix — Analyse Depth

Aubrey Mendelow's Stakeholder Matrix (1991) plots stakeholders on two axes: **Power** (the ability to influence the project's success or failure) and **Interest** (the degree to which the stakeholder is interested in or affected by the project). The resulting four quadrants define communication and engagement strategies:

**High Power / High Interest (Key Players)**: actively manage and involve. These stakeholders can make or break the project and are engaged with its progress. Requires regular, detailed, two-way communication and genuine involvement in key decisions.

**High Power / Low Interest (Keep Satisfied)**: manage carefully. These stakeholders can significantly affect the project but are not actively focused on it. Requires sufficient engagement to maintain their support without overloading them with detail they do not want.

**Low Power / High Interest (Keep Informed)**: keep updated. These stakeholders are affected by or interested in the project but have limited power to influence it. Regular, accessible communication maintains their engagement and prevents them becoming advocates for opposition.

**Low Power / Low Interest (Monitor)**: minimal engagement. Monitor for changes in position. Over-engaging these stakeholders wastes communication resource.

At Level 4 Analyse depth: stakeholder position is dynamic, not fixed. A stakeholder in the Monitor quadrant may move to Key Player if circumstances change, a project that initially appears to have no union implications may acquire them if scope expands to affect working conditions. The stakeholder matrix must be reviewed regularly throughout the project lifecycle, not completed once at Planning and filed.

The analytical extension: stakeholders with high power and currently low interest who move to opposition become the most dangerous stakeholder type, they have the power to block the project and, having not been engaged proactively, have unaddressed concerns that have had time to harden into resistance. Proactive management of the Keep Satisfied quadrant prevents this scenario.

## Risk Management in Projects

At Level 4, risk management connects to the Mendelow analysis. Stakeholder opposition is a risk category, a stakeholder in the Key Players quadrant who loses confidence in the project can trigger scope revision, resource withdrawal, or project cancellation. The risk register at Level 4 includes: risk identification (what could go wrong?); probability assessment (how likely?); impact assessment (how significant?); and mitigation action (what will be done to reduce probability or impact?). Risk response strategies: Avoid (eliminate the risk by changing the plan), Reduce (take action to lower probability or impact), Transfer (pass the risk to a third party, insurance, contract clause), Accept (acknowledge the risk and manage the consequences if it occurs).

## Pass / Merit / Distinction

**Pass:** Project lifecycle stages are described and applied to a project. Gantt chart is included. Mendelow's matrix is applied to identify stakeholders. A risk register is provided.

**Merit:** Critical path is identified in the Gantt chart and analysed. Mendelow's matrix positions are justified with reasoning. Risk register includes mitigation actions appropriate to each risk. The lifecycle stages are connected to each other, what happens in Planning informs what is monitored in Execution.

**Distinction, worked example:** "Applying Mendelow's matrix to the EPR implementation project reveals a dynamic stakeholder risk that the initial matrix does not capture: two Consultant physicians (currently assessed as High Power / Low Interest and placed in Keep Satisfied) have the potential to shift rapidly to High Power / High Opposition if the project's planned system limitations in the outpatient documentation module become apparent during the user acceptance testing phase. The mitigation is proactive engagement before UAT, moving these stakeholders from Keep Satisfied to early involvement by inviting them to the system design review. Their power to influence the Trust Board's project confidence rating makes them effectively Key Players once they engage with the detail, regardless of their initial interest position. The risk register reflects this: 'Consultant opposition to outpatient module, Probability: Medium; Impact: High; Mitigation: Engage both Consultants in design review prior to UAT to identify and address documentation concerns before they crystallise into Board-level opposition.'"

## Management Report Format for CMI Unit 407

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Executive Summary | 150–200 words; project objective, key findings, recommendations |
| Introduction | Project context, scope, and report structure |
| Section 1 | Project Lifecycle: four stages applied; initiation rationale; critical path |
| Section 2 | Project Plan: WBS table; Gantt chart (or appendix reference); critical path analysis |
| Section 3 | Stakeholder Management: Mendelow's matrix; communication plan |
| Section 4 | Risk Management: risk register; mitigation strategies |
| Conclusion | Summary and key recommendations |
| Appendices | Gantt chart; WBS; detailed risk register |
| References | 8–10 Harvard-format sources |

Word count: 2,000–3,000 words (management report format). Note: Unit 407 is a management report, not a structured essay.

## Common Questions About CMI Unit 407

**Does my project have to be a real, ongoing project at work?**
Most Unit 407 briefs require or strongly prefer a real project, a project you are managing, have recently managed, or are planning to manage in your workplace. The applied nature of the unit (producing a project plan with tools) is most effectively assessed through a real scenario. If your current role does not involve formal project management, a proposed project (a team initiative, a quality improvement, a process change) can be used, the assessment is about applying the planning tools, not about reporting on a completed project.

**Does the Gantt chart count toward the word count?**
Gantt charts, risk registers, WBS tables, and other project tools included as appendices do not typically count toward the word count. The body of the management report should reference and analyse these tools, "the Gantt chart (Appendix 1) identifies three tasks on the critical path: X, Y, and Z, all of which are dependent on the external supplier's delivery in Week 4." Include the tools and analyse them; the analysis is assessed, not the tools themselves.

**What is the difference between risk probability and risk impact?**
Probability is how likely the risk is to occur, typically rated Low, Medium, or High (or scored 1–5 in more formal risk management). Impact is how significant the consequences would be if the risk occurred, also rated Low, Medium, or High. A High Probability / Low Impact risk (e.g., minor delays to non-critical tasks) requires different management response than a Low Probability / High Impact risk (e.g., key sponsor withdrawal). Most risk registers include a risk score (probability × impact), and the highest-scoring risks receive the most intensive mitigation planning.

**Can I use PRINCE2 or PMP methodology in Unit 407?**
Yes. PRINCE2 (Projects IN Controlled Environments) and PMI's PMBOK (Project Management Body of Knowledge) are both recognised project management methodologies that can be referenced in Unit 407. PRINCE2's structured approach to project governance, stage boundaries, and product descriptions provides strong analytical content at Level 4. At Evaluate depth: evaluate whether the level of PRINCE2 governance is proportionate to the scale and complexity of your project, a small team project does not require full PRINCE2 structure; a significant IT implementation does. Cite PRINCE2 as: AXELOS (2017) *Managing Successful Projects with PRINCE2*. 6th edn. The Stationery Office.

**How specific does the stakeholder analysis need to be in Unit 407?**
Stakeholder analysis at Level 4 must be specific enough to demonstrate that the matrix has been populated based on actual assessment of real stakeholders, not a generic template. Name stakeholder types (not individuals if confidential), "Trust Board Sponsor," "Clinical Lead, Emergency Medicine," "Union Representative," "IT Department", and justify their quadrant placement with reasoning. The evaluation of the most critical stakeholder relationship (Key Players who require active management) should identify the specific concerns, interests, or power dynamics that make them critical, and the communication approach should be calibrated to those specifics.

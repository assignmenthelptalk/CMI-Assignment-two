## What CMI Unit 517 Covers

CMI Unit 517, Understanding and Leading Organisational Change, is the organisational-level change leadership unit. Where Unit 506 (Managing Change) focuses on change management processes and individual readiness, Unit 517 focuses on organisational change leadership, how leaders understand the forces driving change, align the organisation for change, and manage the cultural and political dimensions of large-scale transformation.

The command verb is Evaluate, you must assess the quality of leadership decisions and their impact on the change outcome, not summarise change management theory.

## PESTLE — Understanding the Drivers for Change

Before evaluating how change is led, evaluate why change is needed. PESTLE identifies external forces creating the pressure or imperative for change:

**Political**: Government policy, regulation, political leadership changes, NHS restructuring under the Health and Care Act 2022, procurement regulation changes (Procurement Act 2023).

**Economic**: Cost pressures, investment availability, market conditions, NHS efficiency targets, commercial sector margin pressures, cost-of-living workforce impacts.

**Social**: Demographic shifts, changing workforce expectations, changing customer/patient behaviours, ageing population demand, hybrid working expectations, patient digital literacy growth.

**Technological**: Digital transformation, automation, clinical technology innovation, electronic health records, AI diagnostic tools, telemedicine.

**Legal**: Employment law, health and safety legislation, sector-specific regulation, GDPR, CQC standards, employment tribunal risk.

**Environmental**: Sustainability and Net Zero commitments, NHS Net Zero targets, ESG reporting, supply chain sustainability.

*At Evaluate depth*: Evaluate which PESTLE forces are most significant for the specific change scenario. Not all forces are equally important, identify the dominant driver(s) and evaluate whether the change leadership response adequately addresses the most significant forces.

## McKinsey 7-S Model — Organisational Alignment During Change

The McKinsey 7-S model (Peters and Waterman, *In Search of Excellence*, Harper & Row, 1982) identifies seven interdependent elements of organisational design. Effective change requires alignment across all seven, changing one element without aligning the others produces dysfunction.

**Hard Ss** (easier to define and change):

**Strategy**: The plan for achieving competitive advantage or organisational purpose. Does the change align with the strategy, or does it require a strategic reorientation?

**Structure**: How the organisation is organised, reporting lines, divisions, team configuration. Does the change require structural adjustment? If structure is not aligned to the new strategy, the old structure will enforce old behaviours.

**Systems**: Processes, IT systems, reporting mechanisms, information flows. Systems that support old ways of working actively resist change, if the performance management system still incentivises pre-change behaviours, people will revert to what is measured.

**Soft Ss** (harder to change but often more important):

**Shared Values (Superordinate Goals)**: The core beliefs and culture of the organisation, what the organisation stands for. The central element in the 7-S model, all other elements should align to shared values.

**Style**: Leadership style and management approach. Does the leadership style adopted during the change match what the change requires, collaborative and empowering, or directive and authoritative?

**Staff**: People, the right people with the right capabilities for the changed organisation. Does the change create a capability gap that recruitment or development must address?

**Skills**: Competencies and capabilities present in the organisation. Not the same as Staff, a well-staffed organisation may lack specific skills (e.g., data analytics, digital project management) that the change requires.

*At Evaluate depth*: Apply the 7-S model to diagnose where alignment gaps exist. The most common change failure pattern at 7-S level: Strategy and Structure are changed but Systems, Skills, and Shared Values remain aligned to the old organisation, the change is announced but the organisation continues running on old operating logic.

**Limitation for Distinction**: The 7-S model does not provide a change sequence or prioritisation, it describes what needs to be aligned but not in what order. In practice, changing all seven elements simultaneously is not feasible. Leaders must prioritise which misalignments are most critical to address first. The Hard Ss are typically changed first (they are more controllable), but the Soft Ss, particularly Shared Values and Style, are usually the most powerful determinants of whether the change actually takes hold.

## Schein (2010) — Organisational Culture and Change Readiness

Edgar Schein's *Organizational Culture and Leadership* (4th edn., Jossey-Bass, 2010) presents organisational culture as having three layers, each with different implications for change:

**Artefacts** (visible surface level): Physical and social manifestations of culture, the office layout, how meetings are run, language and jargon, dress codes, rituals. Visible but easily misinterpreted, what you see does not necessarily reveal why the culture is as it is.

**Espoused Values** (articulated beliefs): What the organisation says it believes, mission statements, values documents, leadership communications. May or may not reflect actual behaviour.

**Basic Underlying Assumptions** (deep, unconscious beliefs): The invisible, taken-for-granted beliefs that actually drive behaviour, how decisions are really made, who really has power, what is actually rewarded. The hardest layer to identify and the most resistant to change.

*At Evaluate depth*: Evaluate the gap between Espoused Values and Basic Underlying Assumptions in the change scenario. An organisation that espouses "innovation and risk-taking" but whose underlying assumptions are "errors are punished and safe decisions are rewarded" will resist an innovation-focused change regardless of the communication programme deployed. This gap between stated and enacted culture is the most significant cultural obstacle to organisational change at leadership level.

## Kotter and Schlesinger (1979) — Managing Resistance

Kotter and Schlesinger (*Harvard Business Review*, 57(2), 1979) identify six approaches to managing resistance to change, each appropriate for different types and sources of resistance:

**Education and communication**: Appropriate when resistance stems from lack of information or misinformation. Educate stakeholders about the reasons for and implications of the change.

**Participation and involvement**: Appropriate when the change initiators lack important information and when stakeholders have significant power. Involving resistors in planning the change reduces resistance and improves the change plan.

**Facilitation and support**: Appropriate when resistance stems from adjustment difficulties, providing coaching, counselling, training, and emotional support to those struggling with the change.

**Negotiation and agreement**: Appropriate when powerful stakeholders have something significant to lose. Offer incentives in exchange for support.

**Manipulation and co-optation**: Using selective information sharing or buying off key resistors with nominal involvement. Risky, if perceived as manipulation, creates deeper resistance and damages trust.

**Explicit and implicit coercion**: Using formal authority, threats, or force. Appropriate only when speed is critical and the change initiators have significant power. Creates resentment and underground resistance if used unnecessarily.

*At Evaluate depth*: Evaluate which approaches were used in the change scenario and whether they were appropriate for the nature and source of resistance. The most common error: using Manipulation when Participation was the appropriate approach, producing initial compliance that erodes into active resistance once the manipulation is recognised.

## CMI Unit 517 — Common Questions

**What is the difference between CMI Unit 506 and CMI Unit 517?**

Unit 506 (Managing Change) focuses on the operational change management process, Kotter's 8 steps, Lewin's force field, ADKAR individual readiness. Unit 517 (Understanding and Leading Organisational Change) focuses on organisational-level change leadership, the strategic drivers (PESTLE), organisational alignment (7-S), cultural readiness (Schein), and political management of resistance (Kotter & Schlesinger). Unit 506 is about managing the change process; Unit 517 is about understanding and leading the organisational conditions that determine whether change can succeed.

**What is McKinsey 7-S used for in CMI Unit 517?**

The 7-S model diagnoses organisational alignment during change, evaluating whether all seven elements (Strategy, Structure, Systems, Shared Values, Style, Staff, Skills) are aligned to support the change or whether some elements remain aligned to the old organisation. A change that updates Strategy and Structure but leaves Systems and Shared Values unchanged will produce an organisation that says it has changed but behaves as it always has.

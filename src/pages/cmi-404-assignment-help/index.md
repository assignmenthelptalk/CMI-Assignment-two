## What CMI Unit 404 Covers

Unit 404 addresses change management as a management responsibility at department and team level. The learning outcomes require you to analyse the forces driving and resisting a specific change, apply change management frameworks to plan or evaluate a change implementation, and evaluate which change approach is most appropriate for the context. At Level 4, both Lewin and Kotter must be applied analytically rather than described, and an evaluative conclusion about their relative appropriateness must be reached.

## Lewin's Force Field Analysis and Three-Step Model

Kurt Lewin's change theory provides two analytically distinct but connected contributions: the Force Field Analysis (1951) as a diagnostic tool, and the three-step Unfreeze-Change-Refreeze model as a change process framework.

**Force Field Analysis**: any situation is maintained in a state of quasi-stationary equilibrium by the balance of driving forces (pressures toward change) and restraining forces (pressures maintaining the status quo). Change occurs when this equilibrium is disrupted, either by increasing driving forces or, more effectively per Lewin, by reducing restraining forces. Reducing restraining forces produces less resistance and less counter-pressure than adding driving force, pushing harder against resistance typically generates equal and opposite resistance.

At Level 4 Analyse depth: analyse the specific driving and restraining forces in a change scenario. Driving forces may include: regulatory requirements, competitive pressure, technological change, financial imperatives, leadership mandate, or market opportunity. Restraining forces may include: habit and routine, fear of the unknown, skill gaps, loss of perceived status, lack of trust in leadership, or inadequate resources for the transition. The analysis should distinguish between the force's source (what is generating the pressure?) and its strength (how powerful is it relative to other forces?).

At Evaluate depth: evaluate which restraining force is most significant for the specific change, the one whose reduction would most shift the equilibrium toward change, and what management action would address it. A change resisted primarily because of skill deficit requires different management response than one resisted because of distrust of the change rationale.

**Lewin's Three-Step Model** (Unfreeze-Change-Refreeze): **Unfreeze:** destabilise the current equilibrium to create openness to change; this requires communicating the need for change, addressing the psychological safety concerns that resistance represents, and reducing the attachment to the current state. **Change:** implement the new behaviour, structure, or process; provide support, training, and leadership visibility during the transition. **Refreeze:** stabilise the new state so that the changed behaviours become the new norm rather than reverting to the previous equilibrium; embedding through policy, structure, reward, and recognition.

At Level 4 Analyse depth: examine what is required at each step for the specific change scenario. A change that moves to the Change step without completing Unfreeze will encounter resistance that is not addressed because it has not been acknowledged, the change is implemented on top of unresolved concerns. A change that completes Unfreeze and Change but fails to Refreeze will regress, the new behaviours are abandoned when the change management focus moves on.

## Kotter's 8-Step Change Model

John Kotter's 8-Step Change Model (1996, *Leading Change*, Harvard Business School Press) provides a sequential process for leading large-scale organisational change: (1) Create Urgency; (2) Build a Guiding Coalition; (3) Form a Strategic Vision and Initiatives; (4) Enlist a Volunteer Army; (5) Enable Action by Removing Barriers; (6) Generate Short-term Wins; (7) Sustain Acceleration; (8) Institute Change.

At Level 4 Analyse depth: analyse what each step contributes to the change process and where the model has particular relevance for the specific scenario. Step 2 (Build a Guiding Coalition) is analytically significant: Kotter argues that change led by a single leader, regardless of their positional authority, is structurally fragile. A coalition of influential supporters from different parts of the organisation provides the political base that makes change sustainable. Step 6 (Generate Short-term Wins) addresses the psychological challenge of sustaining commitment through the difficult middle of a change process, visible early successes counteract the doubt and fatigue that typically emerge after the initial urgency has dissipated.

At Evaluate depth: evaluate whether Kotter's model or Lewin's model is more appropriate for the specific change scenario. Kotter's model is most applicable to large-scale, planned, top-down change in complex organisations where building broad commitment and managing political resistance is critical. Lewin's model is most applicable to smaller-scale change at team or department level, where the primary challenge is understanding and managing resistance rather than building organisational-level political coalitions.

## Comparing Lewin and Kotter

The analytical comparison that earns Distinction at Level 4 identifies what each model reveals that the other does not. Lewin provides the diagnostic tool, Force Field Analysis reveals the specific forces that must be addressed. Kotter provides the process architecture, the sequential steps that manage resistance, build commitment, and institutionalise change at scale. Neither provides a complete account alone: Lewin's three-step model does not provide sufficient process detail for complex large-scale change; Kotter's eight-step model does not provide a diagnostic framework for understanding what specifically is driving resistance.

## Pass / Merit / Distinction

**Pass:** Force Field Analysis applied to a change scenario. Lewin's three-step model described and applied. Kotter's eight steps applied. A workplace change example is included.

**Merit:** Force Field Analysis identifies specific driving and restraining forces with their relative strength assessed. Lewin's three steps are analysed, what is required at each step for the specific scenario. Kotter's steps are connected to the political and human dimensions of the change. A comparative evaluation of Lewin vs Kotter is attempted.

**Distinction, worked example:** "Force Field Analysis of the EPR implementation reveals that the primary restraining forces are not technical capability deficit (which can be addressed through training) but two interconnected psychological forces: loss of perceived clinical autonomy (the system imposes structured documentation that clinicians experience as reducing professional discretion) and lack of trust in management's ability to manage the transition (based on previous unsuccessful system changes in the organisation's history). Reducing these specific restraining forces requires Lewin's Unfreeze work before Kotter's Step 1 (Urgency) will land, clinicians who do not trust leadership's competence in change management will experience urgency messaging as pressure rather than motivation. The most effective sequencing for this specific change is: Lewin's Unfreeze (addressing the trust and autonomy concerns through clinical co-design of the implementation approach) before Kotter's Steps 1-4 (urgency, coalition, vision, volunteers), because the coalition required for Kotter's Step 2 cannot be built with clinicians whose primary concern (autonomy loss) has not yet been acknowledged and addressed."

## Common Questions About CMI Unit 404

**Is Kotter's model from 1996 or 2014, which version should I use?**
John Kotter published the original 8-Step Model in *Leading Change* (1996, Harvard Business School Press). In 2014, he updated the model with revisions to the steps, most notably Step 3 was updated from "Develop a Vision and Strategy" to "Form a Strategic Vision and Initiatives" and Step 4 from "Communicate the Change Vision" to "Enlist a Volunteer Army." At Level 4, either version is acceptable if cited accurately. The 2014 revision is the more current, but many Level 4 materials reference the original 1996 version. Use whichever version your course materials reference and cite it accurately: Kotter, J.P. (1996) *Leading Change*. Harvard Business School Press; or Kotter, J.P. (2014) *Accelerate*. Harvard Business Review Press.

**Do I need to complete all eight Kotter steps for every change, or can I just apply the most relevant ones?**
At Level 4 Analyse depth, you do not need to describe every step in equal detail. Identify the two or three steps most relevant to the specific change scenario and analyse those in depth, while acknowledging the full eight-step model. Step 1 (Urgency) and Step 2 (Coalition) are typically the most analytically rich for most change scenarios, urgency creation addresses the Lewin Unfreeze challenge; coalition building addresses the political dimension that Lewin's model does not specifically capture.

**What is the Force Field in Lewin's Force Field Analysis?**
The "field" in Force Field Analysis is borrowed from physics, specifically Kurt Lewin's earlier work in field theory, which applied physics concepts (forces acting on objects) to psychological and social systems (forces acting on human behaviour). The field is the total psychological and social environment in which the change is occurring, all the forces, pressures, relationships, and influences that shape whether the change equilibrium shifts or holds. At Level 4, you do not need to engage with the physics analogy in depth, understanding that the Force Field Analysis maps the pressures toward and against change, and that reducing restraining forces is more effective than adding driving forces, is the analytically productive application.

**How is Unit 404 different from Level 5 change management units (506 and 517)?**
Unit 404 at Level 4 applies Lewin and Kotter at Analyse and Evaluate depth, the student analyses what the frameworks reveal about the change scenario and evaluates which is more appropriate. Level 5 Unit 506 (Managing Change) applies additional frameworks (ADKAR, Mendelow stakeholder matrix) and requires justifying recommendations. Level 5 Unit 517 (Understanding and Leading Organisational Change) adds Schein's culture model and Kotter & Schlesinger's resistance management approaches at Evaluate depth, and the paper is a management report rather than an essay. Level 7 Unit 709 (Leading Strategic Change) critically analyses the academic debate between Beer & Nohria, Weick, and Pettigrew, a categorically different intellectual engagement.

**Can I write Unit 404 about a change I was affected by rather than one I led?**
Yes. Unit 404 can be written about a change you observed, participated in as a team member, or led. The requirement is that the change scenario is real and specific enough to ground the Force Field Analysis, you need to know what the driving and restraining forces were and what the management approach was. If writing about a change you observed rather than led, frame the evaluation as an assessment of the approach taken, with conclusions about what could have been done differently and why.

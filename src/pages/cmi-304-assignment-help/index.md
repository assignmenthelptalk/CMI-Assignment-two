## What CMI Unit 304 covers

According to the CMI Level 3 syllabus (October 2024), Unit 304 is worth 5 credits, with 18 guided learning hours and 50 hours of total unit time. It has 4 learning outcomes and 9 assessment criteria, and every criterion has to be met for the unit to be passed.

Keywords the syllabus lists for the unit: Communication types, channels, objectives, audience, methods, stages, communication cycle, transmit, respond, feedback, process, planning, effectiveness, communication tools, barriers, legislation and regulation, formal and informal communication.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

### Learning outcome 1: Know the channels and types of communication used in the workplace

- 1.1 **Explain** the importance of effective communication in the workplace
- 1.2 **Identify** channels of communication used in the workplace
- 1.3 **Assess** strengths and weaknesses of different types of communication used in the workplace
- 1.4 **Summarise** legal requirements and organisational policies that affect the use of the different types of communication in the workplace

### Learning outcome 2: Understand the communication cycle

- 2.1 **Explain** the communication cycle and its application in the workplace
- 2.2 **Explain** barriers to effective communication
- 2.3 **Explain** ways to overcome barriers to communication

### Learning outcome 3: Be able to plan communication for a target audience

- 3.1 **Explain** the planning process for communicating with a target audience

### Learning outcome 4: Know how to measure the effectiveness of communication with a target audience

- 4.1 **Assess** ways of measuring the effectiveness of communication with a target audience

## What the command verbs ask for in this unit

The first word of each criterion tells you the depth required. The verbs used in Unit 304 are:

- **Explain** (1.1, 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 3.1): say how and why, not just what.
- **Identify** (1.2): name or pick out the relevant items and say why they matter.
- **Assess** (1.3, 4.1): weigh up the importance, extent or effect of something and make a judgement.
- **Summarise** (1.4): give the key points in a short, accurate form.

These are plain-English readings of the verbs. Your centre's assessment brief and its assessor have the final say on what counts as a pass.

## How to approach a Unit 304 assignment

- Pick two or three channels, such as email, face to face and a team briefing, and assess the strengths and weaknesses of each for AC1.3.
- Use one real message to run through the communication cycle in AC2.1, show the barriers in AC2.2 and the ways round them in AC2.3.
- LO3 and LO4 pair naturally. Plan communication for one target audience (AC3.1), then say how you would measure its effectiveness (AC4.1), for example through feedback or checking understanding.

## Where Unit 304 assignments go wrong

- Naming channels in AC1.2 and not tying them to the workplace. The criterion is about the channels used at work.
- Summarising the law in AC1.4 without linking it to organisational policies on the types of communication you chose.
- Giving one measure of effectiveness in AC4.1. The criterion says assess ways, plural, so compare more than one.

## How CMI suggests Unit 304 can be assessed

CMI says all assessment criteria must be met, and that the following ways of assessing the unit are guidance only. Your centre chooses the format, so follow your own brief:

- Respond to a scenario based on how you would manage communication in the workplace
- Write a report based on the principles of communication in the workplace
- Draw upon your own experience of managing communication within your own organisation
- Create a guide on how to plan and manage communication in the workplace

CMI Level 3 qualifications are graded Pass or Refer. The guideline word count for this unit is 2,000 to 2,500 words, with a 10% allowance above or below, so plan your sections around that.

## CMI Unit 304 FAQ

**Do I have to use the Shannon and Weaver model in Unit 304?**

No. The indicative content names Schramm and Shannon and Weaver as examples of communication cycle models. You may use another recognised model.

**What is the difference between a channel and a type of communication?**

In the indicative content, channels are routes such as face to face, telephone and digital media, while types include written forms such as letters and reports and verbal forms such as calls and briefings.

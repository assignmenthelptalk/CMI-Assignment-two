## What CMI Unit 405 Covers

Unit 405 addresses management communication as both a technical process and a relational skill. ## The Shannon-Weaver Communication Model — Analyse Depth

Claude Shannon and Warren Weaver's Mathematical Theory of Communication (1949) provides the foundational model for analysing communication as a transmission process. The model identifies six components: **Sender** (who originates the message); **Encoder** (the process of converting the idea into transmittable form, words, tone, images); **Channel** (the medium through which the message travels, spoken, written, digital, face-to-face); **Decoder** (the recipient's process of interpreting the received signal); **Receiver** (who receives and interprets the message); and **Noise** (any interference that distorts the signal between sender and receiver).

At Level 4 Analyse depth: analyse how each component contributes to or undermines communication effectiveness in a management context. The Channel selection is analytically significant: Shannon-Weaver was originally developed for telecommunications, where channel choice is primarily a technical decision. In management communication, channel selection is a strategic communication decision. A manager communicating team restructuring via email (low-richness channel) when the team members need to process emotional information (high media richness required) has chosen a channel inappropriate for the message's complexity and emotional weight, the channel creates noise even if the words are carefully chosen.

The feedback loop is the model's most important extension for management application. The original Shannon-Weaver model did not include feedback; Weaver added it in subsequent revisions to address communication as a two-way process. Effective management communication requires feedback loops: confirmation that the message was received as intended, not just transmitted. A team briefing where the manager speaks for 20 minutes without seeking feedback has completed the transmission but not confirmed the communication.

At Evaluate depth: evaluate the most significant source of noise in the specific management communication context. For a healthcare setting, professional hierarchy noise (team members' reluctance to question or challenge senior clinical staff) may be the dominant source of distortion, the message arrives decoded through a deference filter that changes its meaning. The evaluation should identify the specific noise source and conclude with what channel or encoding adjustment would most effectively address it.

## Mehrabian's 7-38-55 Rule — Analyse Depth

Albert Mehrabian's research (1971, *Silent Messages*, Wadsworth) produced the influential but frequently misunderstood finding that, in face-to-face communication, words account for 7% of the overall message, tone of voice accounts for 38%, and body language accounts for 55% of the message's emotional impact.

At Level 4 Analyse depth: the critical analytical contribution is the correct contextual application of Mehrabian's finding. The 7-38-55 proportions apply specifically to the communication of **feelings and attitudes:** particularly when verbal content and non-verbal signals are incongruent. Mehrabian did not argue that words are always 7% important; he demonstrated that when verbal content conflicts with non-verbal signals, the non-verbal signals dominate the emotional message.

This has significant management implications: a manager who verbally praises a team member while displaying flat affect, avoiding eye contact, and speaking in a monotone is communicating a different emotional message through non-verbal channels than through words. The team member's emotional response will be calibrated to the non-verbal (38%+55% = 93%) rather than the verbal (7%). Conversely, a manager who needs their words to carry precise meaning, performance feedback, policy communication, technical instruction, must align non-verbal signals with verbal content, or the emotional noise from incongruence will undermine the message's precision.

At Evaluate depth: evaluate how the specific communication breakdown in the management scenario relates to Mehrabian's channels. If team members consistently express uncertainty about whether the manager's positive feedback is genuine, the issue may be non-verbal incongruence, the 38% tone and 55% body language channels are communicating something different from the 7% verbal channel. The evaluation should conclude with the specific non-verbal adjustments needed and why.

## Communication Barriers — Analyse and Evaluate Depth

Communication barriers at Level 4 are categorised and analysed rather than listed. The four primary barrier categories:

**Physical barriers**: environmental interference, poor acoustics, geographical distance, technology failure, noise in the working environment. For management communication, physical barriers include holding team briefings in spaces with poor acoustics, conducting sensitive conversations without adequate privacy, or relying on text communication for messages that require interpersonal nuance.

**Psychological barriers**: perceptual and cognitive interference, confirmation bias (receiving only information that confirms existing beliefs), emotional state (distress, anxiety, or excitement that narrows attention), status anxiety (the hierarchical filter in management communication), and assumptions about the sender that affect how the message is decoded.

**Semantic barriers**: linguistic interference, jargon, technical language, ambiguous terminology, or cultural differences in word meaning. Particularly significant in cross-disciplinary management communication (a manager using NHS commissioning terminology with a team of non-clinical administrators) or in multicultural team settings.

**Cultural barriers**: differences in communication norms, directness expectations, hierarchy assumptions, and non-verbal conventions between cultural backgrounds. A management communication style that is direct and assertive (valued in some cultural contexts as clarity) may be experienced as dismissive or disrespectful in cultural contexts that value indirect communication and deference to seniority.

## Pass / Merit / Distinction

**Pass:** Shannon-Weaver components are applied to a management communication scenario. Mehrabian's rule is described and applied. Communication barriers are named and related to a workplace example.

**Merit:** Channel selection in Shannon-Weaver is analysed, why a particular channel is or is not appropriate for the message type. Mehrabian's non-verbal incongruence mechanism is analysed, what the 38%+55% channels are communicating when they diverge from the 7% verbal channel. Barriers are analysed at the mechanism level, why a specific barrier type is producing the observed communication failure.

**Distinction, worked example:** "Applying Shannon-Weaver to the performance review communication breakdown: the manager encoded the performance message (below-standard rating) in email (low-richness channel), which eliminated the 55% body language and 38% tone channels (Mehrabian) at precisely the moment when the team member needed to read non-verbal signals to calibrate the emotional significance of the message and to interpret the manager's genuine intent, support or sanction. The email channel created two sources of noise simultaneously: physical (loss of non-verbal channels reducing 93% of emotional signal) and psychological (the team member decoded the email through a status anxiety filter, interpreting the formal channel choice as a signal of impending disciplinary action rather than a developmental conversation). The evaluation conclusion: performance conversations below-standard ratings must use face-to-face channels where the manager can bring the 38% and 55% channels into alignment with the 7% verbal message, and where the feedback loop (Weaver's extension of Shannon's model) enables real-time confirmation of shared understanding."

## Common Questions About CMI Unit 405

**Is Mehrabian's 7-38-55 rule accurate, I've seen it criticised?**
Mehrabian's rule is frequently misquoted as applying to all communication. Mehrabian himself clarified (in his book and subsequently) that the 7-38-55 proportions apply only to the communication of feelings and attitudes, and particularly when verbal and non-verbal channels are incongruent. When verbal content and non-verbal channels are congruent (aligned), the proportions change. At Level 4, demonstrating that you know the correct contextual limitation of Mehrabian's finding, applying it to emotional/attitudinal communication rather than all communication, is the distinction between Analyse and description.

**How is Unit 405 different from Level 3 Unit 304 (Communication in the Workplace)?**
Unit 304 at Level 3 describes how communication works (Shannon-Weaver components) and identifies barriers. Unit 405 at Level 4 analyses what drives communication failure in specific management contexts, examining why channel choices produce the results they do, why non-verbal incongruence undermines verbal messages, why specific barrier types affect specific communication situations differently. The Level 4 standard requires going beyond naming to examining mechanisms and forming evaluative conclusions.

**Can I use a different communication framework instead of Shannon-Weaver?**
Shannon-Weaver is the most commonly assessed communication model at Level 4, but Berlo's SMCR model (Source, Message, Channel, Receiver) is also acceptable and provides similar analytical leverage. Some Unit 405 briefs specify which model to use; most leave the framework choice to the student. Using both Shannon-Weaver and Berlo for comparative analysis is unlikely to add value, choosing one and applying it analytically at Level 4 depth is more effective.

**How does active listening fit into Unit 405 at Level 4?**
Active listening at Level 4 is analysed rather than described. The analytical contribution: active listening is the mechanism by which the feedback loop in Shannon-Weaver's extended model functions. Without active listening behaviours (paraphrasing, summarising, questioning, non-verbal signals of attention), the receiver cannot confirm to the sender that the message was received as intended, and the sender cannot detect whether psychological or semantic noise has distorted the message in transmission. At Evaluate depth: evaluate whether the communication failure in the specific scenario is attributable to a failure of active listening, and if so, which active listening behaviour (paraphrasing, silence, questioning) would most effectively address it.

**What is media richness theory and should I include it in Unit 405?**
Media richness theory (Daft and Lengel, 1986) classifies communication channels by their capacity to carry information: face-to-face is the richest channel (carries verbal, vocal, and visual information simultaneously with immediate feedback), while written communication is the leanest. The theory predicts that high-uncertainty, high-complexity, or emotionally significant messages require richer channels. At Level 4, media richness theory is a strong analytical addition to Shannon-Weaver channel selection analysis, it provides the framework for evaluating why a particular channel is or is not appropriate for the specific message. Cite as: Daft, R.L. and Lengel, R.H. (1986) 'Organizational information requirements, media richness and structural design', *Management Science*, 32(5), pp.554–571.

# CMI Qualification Levels Explained: Levels 3 to 7

CMI (the Chartered Management Institute) offers management and leadership qualifications from Level 3 to Level 8. This guide covers Levels 3 to 7, using CMI's own syllabuses: who each level is designed for, how many units and credits it has, the guideline word counts, the command verbs its criteria use, and what comes next. CMI membership grades (ACMI, MCMI, FCMI) are a separate route, covered in [our guide to CMI membership grades](https://www.cmiassignmenthelp.uk.com/guides/cmi-membership-levels-acmi-mcmi-fcmi/).

## The CMI Qualification Framework

Each CMI qualification is regulated and sits on the Regulated Qualifications Framework (RQF). Each level has an Award, a Certificate and a Diploma, and Levels 5 and 7 also have an Extended Diploma. The size of each is set in credits, and CMI's rules of combination set the minimum:

- **Award**: at least one unit. The minimum is 4 credits at Levels 3 and 5, 6 at Level 4, 5 at Level 6 and 7 at Level 7.
- **Certificate**: at least two units, with a minimum of 13 credits (14 at Level 7).
- **Diploma**: at least 37 credits at Levels 3, 5, 6 and 7. The Level 4 Diploma is 39 credits.
- **Extended Diploma** (Levels 5 and 7 only): at least 54 credits at Level 5 and 64 credits at Level 7.

Your centre decides which units make up your programme, within the rules for the qualification.

| CMI level | Qualification name | Units | Designed for |
|-----------|-------------------|-------|--------------|
| Level 3 | Principles of Management and Leadership | 301 to 321 | Team Leader, Supervisor, Project Officer, Shift Manager, Foreperson |
| Level 4 | Management and Leadership | 4001 to 4009 | Developing management capability, decision-making and managing people |
| Level 5 | Management and Leadership | 501 to 526 | Operations, Departmental and Specialist Managers, typically accountable to a senior manager |
| Level 6 | Professional Management and Leadership Practice | 601 to 614 | Senior Manager, Regional Manager, Specialist Manager, Director |
| Level 7 | Strategic Management and Leadership Practice | 701 to 716 | Senior Manager, Regional Manager, Specialist Manager, Director, CEO |

Each CMI level sits at the RQF level of the same number. In general terms, RQF Level 3 is comparable with A-level, Level 5 with a Higher National Diploma or Foundation Degree, Level 6 with a bachelor's degree and Level 7 with a master's degree. A CMI qualification is a professional qualification, not a university degree.

## CMI Level 3: Principles of Management and Leadership

**Designed for**: practising or aspiring managers who supervise or manage a team to achieve clearly defined outcomes, in roles such as Team Leader, Supervisor, Project Officer, Shift Manager or Foreperson. They set and monitor goals by providing instruction, direction and guidance, and day-to-day operational and project activity is a key part of the role.

**Units**: 301 to 321, 21 units in all, from 301 Principles of Management and Leadership to 321 Managing Own Personal and Professional Development.

**Word count**: the guideline is 2,000 to 2,500 words per unit, with a 10% allowance above or below.

**Command verbs**: Level 3 criteria are led by Explain (112 of 257), then Identify (38), Outline (33), Discuss (27) and Summarise (17). The work is mainly about showing understanding.

**Entry**: centres can offer Level 3 from age 16. CMI sets no entry requirements, and centres must make sure learners have the capability for the level.

**Progression**: CMI points to the Level 5 qualifications as the next step, with the goal of becoming a Chartered Manager.

## CMI Level 4: Management and Leadership

**Designed for**: the development of the role and skill of managers. CMI says the qualifications aim to develop personal management capabilities, make effective use of information in decision-making and operations, and develop skills in managing people.

**Units**: 4001 to 4009, from 4001 Managerial Styles and Behaviours to 4009 Staff Inspection Review. The Diploma also draws on some shared Level 5 units.

**Word count**: the guideline is 3,000 words per unit, with a 10% allowance.

**Command verbs**: Describe (16 of 85), Discuss (12), Identify (11) and Explain (10) lead, with some Evaluate (5) and Analyse (5).

**Entry**: centres can offer Level 4 from age 19.

**Progression**: all successful Level 4 learners are eligible for Foundation Chartered Manager (fCMgr), subject to retaining CMI membership. CMI points to Level 5 as a next step.

## CMI Level 5: Management and Leadership

**Designed for**: practising or aspiring managers and leaders who are typically accountable to a senior manager or business owner. CMI describes the primary role as leading and managing individuals and teams to deliver aims and objectives in line with organisational strategy. Responsibilities may include developing teams, creating operational plans, managing projects, change, finance and resources, and quality.

**Units**: 501 to 526, 26 units in all. A Level 5 Diploma needs at least 37 credits from at least six units. CMI sets some limits on combinations, for example Unit 502 cannot be combined with 503, 505 or 511.

**Word count**: guidelines run from 3,000 to 5,000 words depending on the unit, with a 10% allowance.

**Command verbs**: Analyse (66 of 248), Examine (51) and Evaluate (48) lead, followed by Assess (22), Discuss (21), Develop (10) and Recommend (10). These ask for breaking things down and weighing them, not description.

**Entry**: centres can offer Level 5 from age 16. CMI sets no entry requirements.

**Progression**: all successful Level 5 learners are eligible for Foundation Chartered Manager (fCMgr), subject to CMI membership. Level 5 Diploma learners may be eligible for Chartered Manager (CMgr) with at least three years of management and leadership experience. CMI points to Level 6 as the next qualification.

## CMI Level 6: Professional Management and Leadership Practice

**Designed for**: individuals wishing to develop professional management and leadership practice, with the knowledge, skills and behaviours to drive business activities in a senior management and leadership role. The roles CMI lists are Senior Manager, Regional Manager, Specialist Manager and Director. Level 6 sits between Levels 5 and 7.

**Units**: 601 to 614 (Group A, 14 units). The Diploma can also use Group B units (509, 518, 522 and 525): at least 230 hours of study time must come from Group A, and the rest can come from either group.

**Word count**: the guideline is 3,000 to 4,000 words per unit, with a 10% allowance.

**Command verbs**: the Critically verbs lead. Critically appraise (12 of 75) and Critically assess (9) are the most common, with Discuss (8), Recommend (7), Evaluate (5) and Develop (5) next.

**Entry**: centres can offer Level 6 from age 18.

**Progression**: all successful Level 6 learners are eligible for fCMgr, subject to CMI membership. Diploma learners may be eligible for CMgr with at least three years of management and leadership experience. CMI points to Level 7 as a next step.

## CMI Level 7: Strategic Management and Leadership Practice

**Designed for**: the same senior roles as Level 6, namely Senior Manager, Regional Manager, Specialist Manager and Director, with CEO added. The qualification is for people who will drive business activities in a senior management and leadership role.

**Units**: 701 to 716 (Group A, 16 units), plus Group B units 607, 608, 609, 610, 612 and 614 from Level 6. The Diploma needs at least 37 credits, with at least 30 from Group A. The Extended Diploma needs at least 64 credits, with at least 42 from Group A.

**Word count**: guidelines are 3,500 to 4,500 words per unit, with a 10% allowance.

**Command verbs**: 37 of the 80 criteria begin with Critically, and Critically appraise is the most common (18). Many criteria end in a proposal or strategy, so the work has to judge the evidence and then recommend.

**Entry**: centres can offer Level 7 from age 19.

**Progression**: learners completing the Level 7 Diploma or Extended Diploma may be eligible for Chartered Manager (CMgr) with at least three years of management and leadership experience. CMI points to the Level 8 qualifications in Strategic Direction and Leadership as the next step. ## How CMI Qualifications Are Assessed

Across Levels 3 to 7 the syllabuses say much the same things:

- The grading system is **Pass/Refer**. There is no Merit or Distinction. All assessment criteria must be met.
- Assessment is set by your centre within CMI's recommendations, and the written word is expected to form the majority of assessable work. Group assessment is not a recognised method.
- Each unit has a guideline word count, with a 10% allowance above or below. CMI asks that appendices be kept to a minimum.
- Learners sign a Declaration of Authenticity confirming the work is their own.

Format is not fixed by CMI. Reports, accounts, reflective pieces and other written forms all appear, depending on your centre and brief. ## CMI Award, Certificate, Diploma or Extended Diploma

| Size | Minimum credits | Notes |
|------|----------------|-------|
| Award | 4 to 7 depending on level | A single unit is enough |
| Certificate | 13 (14 at Level 7) | At least two units |
| Diploma | 37 (39 at Level 4) | At least six units at Level 5. Eligible for CMgr at Levels 5, 6 and 7 with three years' experience |
| Extended Diploma | 54 at Level 5, 64 at Level 7 | Levels 5 and 7 only |

You can start with an Award and top up to a Certificate or Diploma later. Total Qualification Time is CMI's estimate of the hours a typical learner spends studying. It is 370 hours for the Level 3, 5, 6 and 7 Diplomas and 390 hours for the Level 4 Diploma, so how long it takes you depends on how many hours a week you can give it and your centre's schedule.

## CMI Membership Is Separate From the Qualification

A CMI qualification and a CMI membership grade are two different things. The qualification is what you study and are assessed on. The membership grade (ACMI, MCMI, FCMI) is a professional post-nominal you hold by meeting CMI's criteria and paying an annual fee. In short:

- **ACMI (Associate):** open to people who have completed a CMI Certificate or Diploma through an approved centre.
- **MCMI (Member):** a degree-level management qualification plus 3 years' management experience, or 5 years' management experience without a qualification.
- **FCMI (Fellow):** 10 years' management experience, at least 3 at a strategic level.
- **CMgr (Chartered Manager):** the MCMI or FCMI criteria plus CMI's Chartered Manager Assessment, written as "CMgr MCMI" or "CMgr FCMI".

People often search for an "MCMI qualification" or an "FCMI qualification", but MCMI and FCMI are membership grades rather than qualifications. The [MCMI qualification and FCMI qualification explained](https://www.cmiassignmenthelp.uk.com/guides/cmi-membership-levels-acmi-mcmi-fcmi/) page sets out what each grade requires.

Foundation Chartered Manager (fCMgr) and Chartered Manager (CMgr) are also linked to the qualifications at Levels 4 to 7, as described under each level above. The full criteria, fees and a comparison table are in [MCMI, FCMI and ACMI explained: CMI membership grades](https://www.cmiassignmenthelp.uk.com/guides/cmi-membership-levels-acmi-mcmi-fcmi/).

## CMI Qualification Levels — Common Questions

**What level of CMI should I study?**

Match the level to the scope of your role, using the roles CMI names for each level. Team Leader or Supervisor roles fit Level 3. Operations, Departmental or Specialist Manager roles, accountable to a senior manager, fit Level 5. Senior Manager, Regional Manager, Specialist Manager or Director roles fit Levels 6 and 7. Your centre or employer will confirm the right level, and if your employer is funding the programme, ask which level it is set at before enrolling.

**Is CMI Level 7 equivalent to a Master's degree?**

CMI Level 7 sits at Level 7 of the RQF, the same level as a master's degree. It is a professional qualification, not an academic award, so it does not carry the same credential or international brand as a university degree or MBA. Its value is in UK management practice and the Chartered Manager route.

**What is the difference between CMI Level 5 and Level 7?**

Level 5 is for managers accountable to a senior manager who lead and manage teams to deliver strategy. Level 7 is for senior roles driving business activities across an organisation. In criteria, Level 5 is led by Analyse, Examine and Evaluate, while Level 7 criteria begin with Critically in 37 of 80 cases and often end in a proposal or strategy. See [Level 5 vs Level 7](https://www.cmiassignmenthelp.uk.com/guides/cmi-level-5-vs-level-7/).

**How long does a CMI Level 5 Diploma take?**

CMI's estimate is at least 370 hours of Total Qualification Time, across at least six units. Calendar time depends on your centre's schedule and your weekly study hours, so ask your centre for its timetable.

**What are ACMI, MCMI and FCMI?**

They are professional membership grades of CMI, used as post-nominals after your name. ACMI follows completion of a CMI Certificate or Diploma through an approved centre. MCMI and FCMI depend on years of management experience, and for MCMI a degree-level management qualification. Read the [full explanation of each grade](https://www.cmiassignmenthelp.uk.com/guides/cmi-membership-levels-acmi-mcmi-fcmi/).

**Can I get help with my CMI assignment at any level?**

Yes. The service covers Level 3 to Level 7. Each level has different command verbs and criteria, so support is matched to the level and unit you are studying.
